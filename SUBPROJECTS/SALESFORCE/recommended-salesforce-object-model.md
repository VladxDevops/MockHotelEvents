# Recommended Salesforce Object Model

## Purpose
This document defines a practical Salesforce data model for the mock hotel wedding planning project. The objective is to model the sales and inquiry lifecycle while keeping Salesforce in its proper boundary: customer and sales context, not the system of record for venue inventory or final operational booking execution.

## Design principles
- Keep Salesforce focused on customer, lead, opportunity, and event request data.
- Use custom objects for the wedding-specific workflow.
- Keep operational details in downstream systems or a dedicated booking/operations datastore.
- Add integration-friendly identifiers for traceability across Mulesoft, Pub/Sub, Kafka, and cloud services.
- Use status fields consistently so the lifecycle is clear to both sales and operations teams.

## Standard objects

### Lead
Represents the initial inquiry or prospect for a wedding or event.

Recommended fields:
- FirstName
- LastName
- Email
- Phone
- Company
- LeadSource
- Status
- EventType
- PreferredEventDate
- AlternateEventDate
- EstimatedGuestCount
- BudgetRange
- Notes
- CreatedDate

### Contact
Represents the couple, planner, or primary decision maker.

Recommended fields:
- FirstName
- LastName
- Email
- Phone
- RelationshipType
- AccountId

### Account
Represents the customer or organizing party connected to the wedding.

Recommended fields:
- Name
- BillingStreet
- BillingCity
- BillingState
- BillingPostalCode
- BillingCountry
- CustomerCategory

### Opportunity
Represents the sales opportunity for the event.

Recommended fields:
- Name
- AccountId
- PrimaryContactId
- Amount
- StageName
- CloseDate
- EventType
- WeddingDate
- EstimatedGuestCount
- Probability
- OwnerId
- CurrencyIsoCode

Recommended fields:

- Status__c
- Due_Date__c
- Notes__c

## Relationship model
- One Lead can generate one or more Event_Request__c records.
- One Event_Request__c should map to one Opportunity.
- One Event_Request__c can have multiple Venue_Availability__c records.
- One Event_Request__c can have multiple Event_Quote__c records over time.
- One Opportunity can link to one confirmed Event_Booking__c.

## Recommended event flow mapping

1. Lead is created from inquiry
2. Event_Request__c captures detailed event requirements
3. Opportunity records the sales opportunity
4. Venue availability is checked
5. Event_Quote__c is created or updated
6. Event_Booking__c is created after acceptance or approval
7. Internal operations tasks receive the handoff

## Integration-friendly field guidance
- Add `External_Request_Id__c` to map a Salesforce request back to the external inquiry ID.
- Add `External_Booking_Id__c` to map a confirmed booking back to a downstream booking service.
- Store correlation IDs in the payload rather than creating a giant custom object just for message tracking.
- Keep timestamps and status transitions standardized across all objects.

## Recommended starter subset
For the first mock implementation, the leanest useful version is:
- Lead
- Opportunity
- Event_Request__c
- Event_Quote__c
- Event_Booking__c

This is enough to demonstrate a wedding inquiry flow without overbuilding Salesforce.

## Example scenario
A couple submits a wedding inquiry for October with 180 guests and a preferred garden venue. Salesforce creates:
- Lead: new prospect record
- Opportunity: wedding opportunity
- Event_Request__c: event details, guest count, budget, venue preference
- Event_Quote__c: pricing from the availability and pricing workflow
- Event_Booking__c: final confirmation after approval

## Why this model fits the repo
This model is realistic enough for a mock enterprise integration story but intentionally simple enough to understand quickly. It matches the project’s business story:
- customer inquiry enters through Salesforce
- sales qualification happens in Salesforce
- Mulesoft orchestrates validation and enrichment
- Pub/Sub or Kafka carries asynchronous event lifecycle messages
- hotel operations and booking logic remain separate from the CRM system

## Final recommendation
Use this as the baseline model for the repository. It is a good balance of realism, simplicity, and interview-readiness. If additional complexity is needed later, you can expand into more detailed custom objects for room blocks, vendor coordination, banquet requirements, and operational staffing.
